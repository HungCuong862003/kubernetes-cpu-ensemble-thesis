## Critical finding flagged — Bitbrains dominates the primary metric

The unweighted-mean primary metric (1.922) is decomposed as:
- Alibaba contribution: 0.149 (8% of primary)
- **Bitbrains contribution: 1.620 (84% of primary)**
- ByteDance contribution: 0.153 (8% of primary)

This is a scale artifact, not a quality difference. Bitbrains CPU values are roughly 10× larger in absolute terms (Bitbrains cpu_target ~18–25; Alibaba ~3–5; ByteDance ~28–68). Pinball loss is not scale-invariant.

### Implication for F3 outcome interpretation

- LoRA improving only Bitbrains by 5% → hits SUCCESS even if Alibaba/ByteDance stay flat
- LoRA improving Alibaba+ByteDance by 10% but Bitbrains 0% → headline ~0.8% (FAILURE) despite genuinely helping 2/3 datasets

The pre-registered criterion has this design flaw. We knew the datasets had different scales when we locked it but didn't propagate that to the primary metric.

## Within-pattern observations

### Pinball monotonically decreases with τ across ALL 11 cells

For every (dataset, horizon), pinball at τ=0.95 < τ=0.9 < τ=0.8 < τ=0.7 ≤ τ=0.5. Mechanism: predictions are biased above truth at high quantiles, and the pinball formula `max(τ·diff, (τ-1)·diff)` produces small absolute values when (τ-1) is small and diff is negative.

This means **Chronos-2 already produces moderately calibrated over-provisioning quantiles out of the box.** The LoRA fine-tune has finite room to improve at high τ.

### Pinball increases with horizon, as expected

Alibaba h=10 → h=120: 0.237 → 0.474 (2× increase at τ=0.9). Bitbrains: 1.535 → 8.015 (5× increase). ByteDance: 0.383 → 0.480 (1.25× increase). Bitbrains shows the largest horizon degradation — consistent with its non-diurnal autocorrelation profile (ACF@24h ≈ 0.12).
